# Describing visible problem areas

Render-style precedence: follow the selected style master. Both cartoon styles use strongly exaggerated facial and body geometry; simple cartoon uses clean simplified surfaces, while high fidelity adds realistic surface detail. Large heads and expressive eyes are deliberate cartoon design choices; avoid chibi, baby-like adult anatomy, or malformed proportions. Keep visible concern shapes readable and surface relief restrained. These notes never authorize changing a master prompt.

Use this vocabulary when a script names a visible concern. Choose a location and two or three strong visual details that belong together. Write the appearance directly in the character prompt; do not present this list to the user or ask them to define an ordinary problem term. Match the script and any reference independently supplied by the user. Exaggerate the relevant shapes further for stylized 3D cartoon while keeping the person and unaffected body parts consistent.

## Cellulite

- **Where:** describe visible cellulite on the upper back and outer thighs and hips. Do not mention buttocks in this user's copyable character prompts. If the script explicitly calls for wider distribution, mention other visible affected areas sparingly. Do not cover the calves, face, or hands by default.
- **Wardrobe:** expose the affected regions: shorts or a short skirt/dress for thighs and hips, a sleeveless or short-sleeved layer for upper arms, and an appropriately cut top if the abdomen is affected. Layer with an open overshirt or jacket that does not hide the skin. Keep the styling natural and age-appropriate; never use nudity to reveal every area at once.
- **Surface:** visibly uneven natural skin texture across broad patches of the upper thighs, with low-relief variation that looks quilted as a whole rather than isolated individual marks. A few shallow creases may cross the upper thighs. Skin remains soft, matte, intact, and natural in tone; thighs retain a full, rounded contour.
- **Tested wording:** The user reports an okay render from: “She has pronounced cellulite across the exposed [skin]. The surface looks uneven and quilted rather than marked by separate spots. A few shallow creases cross the area beneath the buttocks. Her skin remains soft, matte, and intact, and her thighs keep their full, rounded shape.” Correct the first sentence's grammar in new prompts and honor the user's preference not to mention buttocks: say “across the exposed upper thighs” and place the shallow creases “near the tops of her thighs.” Preserve the rest of the tested wording closely.
- **Lessons from failed renders:** long waves and furrows produced draped wrinkles; isolated dents produced circular holes; describing dense raised fat lobules and tightly packed bumps produced repeated scale-like texture across thighs, calves, and arms. The word “quilted” worked when paired with the successful sentence above and no elaborate texture geometry. Do not copy texture from one region across the entire body.
- For stylized 3D cartoon, keep the face and proportions stylized but render cellulite with realistic skin texture and restrained depth. Do not exaggerate the skin relief.
- Keep cellulite as surface texture. Do not turn it into bruises, lesions, swelling, or a single large lump.

## Swollen ankles or feet

- **Wardrobe:** show bare lower calves, ankles, and insteps with a hem well above the ankle and low-cut shoes or simple sandals. No socks or boots. Make the calf-to-foot transition visible, so the swollen section does not read as isolated round knobs poking out beneath trousers.
- **Where and shape:** both lower legs thicken gradually toward the ankles, which look wide and softly rounded with ankle-bone definition reduced. The fullness continues smoothly across the tops of the feet; instep tendons are softened and the low shoes fit snugly. The heel, toes, and foot proportions remain normal. Show some natural skin creasing and, when appropriate, faint blue-purple superficial vessels. The swelling should be clearly visible but integrated with the whole lower leg and foot, rather than projecting as separate spherical side bulges.
- **Readable phrasing:** “Her bare lower calves broaden into visibly swollen ankles with soft, thick contours and little ankle-bone definition. The fullness continues across the tops of her feet, making the insteps puffy and the low shoes fit snugly. Faint blue-purple vessels and gentle skin creases remain visible around the ankles.”
- **Failed render:** “bulging on either side,” “completely hiding ankle bones,” and “high puffy feet pressing tightly” produced giant round lobes beside the ankles and heels. Use continuous fullness and normal foot proportions instead. At full-body scale the ankles still look notably wider than normal, but they remain believable.
- Show one or both sides as the script indicates. Keep the visible vessels distinct from bruises; avoid inventing wounds, dramatic redness, or a diagnosis.

## Bloating and generalized puffiness

- **Where:** the character visually reads as moderately heavier overall, with fullness through the face, jaw and neck, torso, waist, upper arms, hips, and legs as relevant. The abdomen can be uncomfortable, but it is part of the broader silhouette rather than the only enlarged area.
- **Shape:** broaden the waist and soften its definition; add fullness across abdomen, flanks, upper arms, hips, and thighs rather than one forward-pointing round dome. Show a full neck and one broad, soft double-chin area beneath the jaw that obscures its edge. On an older before/problem character, let this single heavy area hang naturally toward the neck, with fine surface wrinkles and lightly creased skin. Do not form several stacked chin rolls or deep horizontal bands. If heavier, the lower cheeks, underside of upper arms, and lower abdomen can also settle and hang with gravity instead of looking round and taut. Clothes feel snug at several points, not just at the stomach. Preserve the person's recognizable features and build.
- **Skin:** retain believable pores, creases, and soft age texture. If thighs or upper arms are exposed and cellulite is independently relevant, use the *tested restrained cellulite wording above* as natural skin detail rather than adding embossed texture everywhere. Do not automatically add cellulite to every bloated character.
- **Readable phrasing:** “She looks noticeably heavier all over, with fuller cheeks, a soft double chin that obscures her jawline, a thicker neck, rounded upper arms, a broader waist, and fuller hips and thighs. Her middle looks full across the front and sides, and her blouse fits snugly at the arms and waist. Her skin retains natural texture and soft creases.”
- Do not create a pregnancy silhouette, giant isolated belly, or extremely obese body unless explicitly specified. The user wants an overall overweight look with bloating, not an extreme transformation in body size.
- Do not add stretch marks as a bloating detail unless the user or script specifically calls for them as a separate, stable feature.
- Describe the visible before look only. Do not assert that apparent weight is fluid trapped under the skin or that a product removes it; appearance alone does not establish a mechanism.

## Stretch marks

- **Where:** lower abdomen, hips, thighs, or upper arms when those areas are visible and relevant.
- **Surface:** fine or broad, slightly wavy, roughly parallel lines that vary in length and direction. They may be pale, silvery, pink, or darker than surrounding skin depending on the character and stage. They follow the curve of the body rather than forming perfect stripes.
- **Readable phrasing:** “Several pale, uneven, gently curved lines fan outward across the lower stomach toward the hips.”
- Stretch marks are lines; cellulite is dimpled texture. Neither is an automatic feature of bloating or a guaranteed after-state change.

## Puffy face and lower jaw

- **Where:** upper lids, under-eyes, cheeks, lower cheeks beside the mouth, beneath the chin, and neck as called for.
- **Shape:** heavy upper lids; raised lower lids with bags and a crease below; rounded cheek fullness; sagging lower cheeks beside the mouth; a full neck with one substantial soft double chin beneath the jaw that hides its outline. For an older before/problem character, describe the single under-chin mass hanging into the upper neck with micro-wrinkles and natural skin grain across its surface. Loose neck skin may be visible below it, but do not turn the chin into multiple deep folds or a stack of rolls. If the character is also heavier, the jowls and under-chin area look fuller and heavier. Make the neck and chin contour prominent when the story's transformation involves perceived weight loss.
- **Readable phrasing:** “The lower lids form thick pouches beneath tired eyes and her cheeks look full. A soft double chin hangs beneath the jaw, hiding its edge and blending into her full neck.”
- Distinguish ordinary age-related jowls or neck folds from temporary puffiness. Preserve stable age features in the after prompt.

## Older heavier before states: gravity and loose skin

- Give the before state a legible age silhouette: lower cheeks sag beside the mouth and **one large, continuous double chin** hangs toward the upper neck. Render tiny wrinkles, pores, and fine creasing on that skin. Neck laxity can soften the contour beneath it, but avoid several chin layers, concentric rolls, or deep banded folds. “Turkey neck” should not override the single double-chin shape.
- When the person is also overweight, show soft weight settling in the lower face, upper arms, and lower abdomen. Exposed upper arms can have fullness hanging from their undersides rather than firm cylindrical volume. A rounded stomach may have a lower apron-like softness, but do not turn it into an extreme overhang unless requested.
- Vary the degree with age, build, and character. An older lean person may have neck laxity without a heavy double chin; a younger heavy person may have fullness without pronounced loose skin. Do not use the same aged face for every character.
- In the after state, reduce the changeable fullness if the script calls for it. The person’s age and appropriate skin creases remain recognizable, but the problem-related double chin can be removed and their face can look brighter and fresher while still their age.

## Full upper arms in weight-loss before states

- For a weight-loss before/problem character, infer visibly full upper arms as part of the overall heavier silhouette when the arms are shown; the script does not need to say “lipedema.” Show both upper arms as broad and heavy relative to narrower forearms. Fullness gathers along the back and underside of each upper arm and may hang softly with age; keep elbows and hands recognizable rather than inflating the entire arm into a tube. Vary intensity with the person's build and narrative.
- Expose the arms with sleeveless or short sleeves so the difference in shape reads. A fitted sleeve edge can press into the upper arm if the outfit calls for it.
- **Readable phrasing:** “Her upper arms are markedly broad and heavy, with soft fullness hanging along their undersides. They taper more noticeably at the elbows into slimmer forearms, and the edge of her short sleeves sits tight against the upper arms.”
- This is a visual design cue, not proof of a condition. Describe the form in copyable appearance prompts rather than asserting a medical diagnosis from arm size alone.

## Other swollen joints

- **Fingers/knuckles:** finger joints look rounded and enlarged, knuckle definition is softened, rings sit tight, and the hand may be held stiffly.
- **Knees:** fullness gathers around or above the kneecap, the usual contours are less defined, a trouser knee or knee brace pulls tight if relevant, and the leg may be held more carefully.
- **Wrists:** rounded fullness obscures wrist bones, and a watch strap or sleeve cuff may press in.
- Describe the exact area and the shape change instead of saying only “swollen joints.” Do not add a diagnosis or make every joint affected.
