# Theory for unique character casting (read this before casting ANY character)

> **This is the casting brain of the whole system.** It decides WHO a character is, in any style: Pixar simple, Pixar high fidelity, realistic, UGC, and any future style (claymation, 2D, the Zack D explainer). Every character, including UGC creators built from real photo references, must pass the Final Editor Check at the end.
> The source page's pictures (feature charts, example portraits, outfit boards) are described in words here. The words are what the prompt needs.

# PURPOSE
We are NOT creating generic characters by only changing ethnicity, skin color, or clothes.
We are creating realistic individuals.
Think about the full person:
- Who are they?
- Where are they from?
- What kind of lifestyle do they have?
- What kind of personality do they have?
- What situation are they in?
Every character should feel like a different real person who could actually be watching our ads, not the same AI character with a different skin tone or outfit.

# Characters should feel familiar, not “Perfect”
The goal is not to create the most conventionally attractive or “perfect” AI person.
The goal is to create someone the viewer could realistically recognize.
They might remind them of:
themselves, their mom, their husband, a coworker, their neighbor, or someone they see every day.
This matters because people tend to respond better to advertising when the person delivering the message feels similar or relatable to them. Research around self-congruity and perceived similarity has linked that feeling of “this person is like me” with stronger ad attitudes, credibility, and purchase intention.
When consumers see videos whose faces were physically more similar to their own, they perceived the reviews as more credible and were more accepting of the recommendation.
That is why small human details matter.
Instead of generating another perfectly symmetrical AI face, give the character something recognizable: slightly hooded eyes, an uneven eyebrow, a stronger nose, gray temples, freckles, tired eyes, a gap between the teeth, sun spots, or another feature that makes them feel like an actual person.

We are not trying to make characters flawless. We are trying to make viewers think:
“I know someone who looks like that.”

# BLACK AMERICAN CHARACTERS
American Black culture is diverse and layered. Older generations (especially those in their 50s and 60s+) often lean more god-fearing and may draw from Christian traditions in language, values, or modest choices when it fits the character — but faith is only one thread. Sports culture runs deep: basketball, American football, and athletic pride shape style, conversation, and identity for many people of all ages. Street style and urban fashion influences are also strong — clean sneakers, intentional layering, jewelry, and other expressive details.
### Variation Requirements
Black characters need real variation in skin tone, face structure, body type, hairstyle, grooming, fashion, accessories, socioeconomic background, regional style, and personality. Possible skin tones range from light brown through medium, rich, deep, and very dark brown. Never reuse one Black person’s face template.

### Black Men – Styling
Typical pieces include heavyweight T-shirts, tank tops, open flannels, short-sleeve overshirts, denim, cargo shorts, relaxed jeans, workwear pants, varsity-style jackets, bombers, and casual polos. Footwear leans toward Jordan-style retro basketball sneakers, clean Nike-style sneakers, high-tops, classic white sneakers, Timberland-style boots, or slides in very casual settings. Sneakers matter most for fashion-conscious, sporty, younger or middle-aged characters, or those into sneaker culture. Do not automatically give every Black man Jordans.
Accessories (depending on personality) can include thin or thicker gold chains, silver chains, watches, stud earrings, sunglasses, baseball caps, or durags — keep them restrained. When tattoos fit, common motifs are crosses, praying hands, Bible verses, angels, family or children’s names, dates, memorial portraits, script lettering, and black-and-gray sleeves. Religious tattoos only appear when the character context supports religion; do not assume every Black character is Christian.

### Black Women – Styling
Depending on personality, Black women often use stronger jewelry (hoop or statement earrings, stacked bracelets, rings), handbags, coordinated shoes, richer colors, expressive hairstyles, and polished grooming. Clothing can include stylish jeans, tailored pants, fitted blouses, knit tops, coordinated casual sets, jumpsuits, cropped or denim jackets, fashionable sneakers, and sandals. Useful colors (kept coordinated, never randomly colorful) include cobalt, emerald, orange, purple, burgundy, rust, red, cream, and mustard.

### Hair
Hair is not an afterthought. Black hair culture is rich, varied, and deeply personal. Getting it right is one of the clearest ways to show cultural respect and make people feel represented.
Different hairstyles have carried different meanings throughout history. Braids and cornrows, for example, have long served practical, social, and cultural purposes — from protective styling and community identity to historical forms of communication and resistance. Locs (dreads) often signal spiritual, cultural, or personal commitment and carry their own history of pride and reclamation. These styles are not interchangeable fashion accessories; they hold weight.
Possible styles include natural curls, Afros, twist-outs, braids, box braids, cornrows, twists, locs, silk presses, bobs, ponytails, short natural cuts, protective styles, and wigs. Texture, length, and finish should vary. Do not default every Black woman (or man) to long straight hair or the same generic look. Age, lifestyle, and personality should influence the choice — a polished silk press, a well-maintained protective style, or natural gray curls can all feel intentional and authentic.

# HISPANIC CHARACTERS
“Latino” or “Hispanic” is not a single race or look. It covers a wide range of backgrounds, skin tones, and facial features shaped by Indigenous, European, African, and mixed ancestries. A Mexican-American character will often read differently from a Puerto Rican, Dominican, Cuban, Colombian, or Central American one.
Skin tones can range from light and European-presenting to olive, medium brown, dark brown, and deep. Faces, hair textures, and body types vary just as widely. Avoid the lazy default of “white face + tan skin.” Specific national or regional background can be used when it helps the story or product, but it should never become a costume.
### Face Design
Latino faces are highly varied. You may see broad rounded faces, long angular ones, strong or soft cheekbones, fuller cheeks, or sharper bone structure. Noses range from prominent and aquiline to rounded, narrow, or broader. Brows are often strong; eyes can be hooded, deep-set, or more open. Jawlines move between square and softer. The goal is natural variation, not a single “Latino face” template.

### Latino Men – Hair & Grooming
Latino men often have stronger facial and body hair growth. Stubble, moustaches, goatees, full beards, and visible chest or arm hair are common and should feel natural rather than over-groomed or removed by default. Grooming can range from very clean and polished to more relaxed, depending on age and personality.
Hair itself varies widely: thick straight hair, waves, curls, tight fades, tapers, textured crops, slicked-back styles, or mature receding hairlines. A fade is frequent but should never be the only option. Older men may show grey at the temples, salt-and-pepper facial hair, or natural thinning without losing presence.

### Latino Men – Clothing & Variation Requirements
Clothing needs real range. Casual and family settings often include fitted T-shirts, polos, short-sleeve patterned or plaid shirts, jeans, chinos, workwear pieces, leather belts, clean sneakers, and boots. Some men dress more sporty or street-influenced; others lean polished or working-class. Accessories such as a chain, watch, ring, or sunglasses can appear when they fit the character

*(Outfit board: an everyday-to-occasion clothing range for this audience.)*

### Latina Women – Hair
Hair is a strong point of identity. Styles can include long waves, straight hair, natural curls, layered cuts, bobs, pulled-back looks, or shorter practical styles. Texture ranges from fine and straight to thick and curly. Older women may show natural gray mixed through darker hair or choose softer, lower-maintenance cuts

### Latina Women – Clothing & Variation Requirements
Everyday clothing includes fitted jeans, blouses, tailored pants, knit tops, matching casual sets, jumpsuits, cardigans, and simple dresses. Footwear ranges from sneakers and sandals to low heels or stylish flats. Accessories such as hoop earrings, bracelets, necklaces, and handbags appear often, but the overall look should feel intentional rather than stacked

# WHITE CHARACTERS
### Cultural Notes
It covers a broad range of European ancestries and regional backgrounds — Northern, Southern, Eastern, and Western European — each with different typical features, skin tones, and styling tendencies. Skin can range from very fair and freckled to olive, ruddy, or lightly tanned. Hair color, eye color, face structure, and body type vary widely.
Avoid falling back on the same default every time: the slim blonde woman, the average brown-haired man with light stubble, or the generic “everyman” face. Age, region, socioeconomic background, and personality should shape the character far more than a single ethnic label. When white characters are given real variation, they stop feeling like background defaults and start feeling like distinct people.

*(Example portrait in the high-fidelity Pixar style: a distinct individual of this audience, varied in age, skin depth, face structure and grooming.)*

### White Men – Hair & Grooming
Hair among white men tends to show more visible color variation than most other groups — natural blondes, light browns, dark browns, auburns, and reds all appear, often with noticeable shifts as they age into gray or white. Texture ranges from fine and straight to thick and wavy or curly. Styles lean practical more often than highly sculpted: short crops, side parts, textured tops, longer length, or simply letting a receding hairline or baldness sit naturally.
Clean-shaven is common, as is light stubble or a full but softer beard. Heavy, dense beards or thick mustaches are less frequent unless the character specifically leans that way. Body hair follows the same pattern — some men have almost none, others have moderate chest or arm hair.

### White Men – Clothing & Variation Requirements
White men’s casual clothing tends to sit in a quieter range than what you often see with Black or Latino styling. Neutrals, muted colors, and simpler silhouettes show up more frequently — plain T-shirts, polos, button-downs, flannels, jeans, chinos, and basic workwear. The overall look is often less about statement pieces and more about comfort or subtle polish.
Accessories stay minimal for most — a watch or wedding ring is usually enough. Chains and heavier jewelry are less common unless the character’s personality specifically calls for them.

*(Outfit board: an everyday-to-occasion clothing range for this audience.)*

### White Women – Hair
Hair varies widely in color, texture, and length. Natural shades include blonde, light brown, dark brown, auburn, red, and black, often with natural variation or gray as women age. Styles range from long and straight or wavy, to layered cuts, bobs, short crops, ponytails, or pulled-back looks. Texture can be fine and straight, thick and wavy, or curly. Do not default every white woman to long straight blonde or brunette hair.

### White Women – Clothing & Variation Requirements
Everyday clothing includes jeans, tailored pants, blouses, knit tops, casual dresses, cardigans, light jackets, and simple sets. Footwear can be sneakers, flats, sandals, boots, or low heels. Some women dress more polished or feminine; others lean casual, sporty, or minimal.
Accessories such as simple earrings, necklaces, watches, or handbags appear, but should never feel forced. Avoid making every white woman slim, blonde, lightly made-up, and dressed in neutral or “safe” clothing. Body type, age, region, and personality should drive the choices — not a single default look.

*(Outfit board: an everyday-to-occasion clothing range for this audience.)*

# AGE MUST ACTUALLY SHOW
If we say:
55-year-old
the character should look 55.
Not:
attractive 36-year-old with gray hair.
## Aging: 30s
Use:
- mild eye lines
- subtle forehead texture
- early smile lines
- natural pores
- slight tiredness
## Aging: 40s
Use:
- crow's feet
- forehead lines
- nasolabial folds
- mild jaw softness
- under-eye bags
- some pigmentation
- occasional gray hair
## Aging: 50s
Use:
- heavier eyelids
- deeper smile lines
- facial volume changes
- beginning jowls
- neck softness
- gray hair
- thinning hair
- pigmentation
- visible skin texture
## Aging: 60+
Use:
- jowls
- deeper facial folds
- under-eye hollows
- heavier eyelids
- gray / silver hair
- thinning hair
- neck laxity
- age spots
- older hands
- posture changes
Allow older people to actually look older.
# TRANSFORMATION RULE
The AFTER person must remain the SAME PERSON.
Do not change:
- skull
- nose
- eyes
- height
- shoulder width
- ethnicity / Skin color
- age
- underlying facial structure
Change only:
- fat
- bloating
- puffiness
- posture
- confidence
- clothing fit
- grooming if relevant

# FINAL EDITOR CHECK
Before approving:
- Does the ethnicity read correctly?
- Is it more than skin color?
- Is the face unique?
- Does the nose have a specific shape?
- Are the eyes specific?
- Does the character look their age?
- Does the hairstyle make sense?
- Does clothing match age?
- Does clothing match culture?
- Does clothing match income?
- Does clothing match the event?
- Do the accessories make sense?
- Are we avoiding generic AI beauty?
- Are we avoiding stereotypes?
- Are older characters still allowed to have personality?
- Are people within the same ethnicity visually different?
- Would we recognize this person again?
If several answers are NO:
redo the character before generating the video.
# FINAL RULE
Every character should feel like a specific person who happens to belong to that audience.
