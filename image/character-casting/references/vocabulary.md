# Visual vocabulary (faces, hair, skin)


Render-style precedence: follow the selected style master. Both cartoon styles use strongly exaggerated facial and body geometry; simple cartoon uses clean simplified surfaces, while high fidelity adds realistic surface detail. Large heads and expressive eyes are deliberate cartoon design choices; avoid chibi, baby-like adult anatomy, or malformed proportions. Keep visible concern shapes readable and surface relief restrained. These notes never authorize changing a master prompt.

Use these as **options**, not a checklist. Pick only a few compatible traits for each person. Describe features in ordinary words; avoid treating facial-feature labels as indicators of ethnicity.

## General feature chart


- Face: oval, round, square, heart, diamond, long rectangular, pear/triangle.
- Nose: prominent curved bridge, straight bridge, rounded tip, broad, narrow, long, short and upturned, or slightly crooked. Use visible physical words rather than the chart's ancestry-associated nose labels.
- Eyes: almond, round, hooded, deep-set, wide-set, close-set, slightly upturned or downturned.
- Brows: full/bushy, thin, straight, softly angled, sharply angled, high arch, rounded.
- Hair: fine/medium/coarse strands; sparse/medium/dense; straight, wavy, curly, or coily. Give style and color in plain language (for example dark brown, auburn, silver at the temples), not numbered curl types or salon shade names unless asked.

## Face vocabulary from the user's real-person examples

The written examples show that a recognizable face comes from the **relationship between features**, visible age, and ordinary personal details. Do not recreate one photographed person's exact likeness unless the user explicitly asks to use that person as a reference. Use these phrases as a vocabulary bank across target audiences, selecting only what fits a particular character:

- **Overall shape:** broad across the cheekbones and narrower at the chin; wide forehead with a compact lower face; long face with a rounded chin; broad lower face and short neck; cheeks that remain full while the temples look flatter.
- **Forehead and brows:** broad smooth forehead; sparse outer brows; brows that angle down toward the nose; a short vertical crease between the brows; two deeper forehead lines that remain visible at rest. Do not make one eyebrow higher or more angled than the other as a distinctive trait.
- **Eyes and surrounding skin:** one eyelid resting lower than the other; upper lids folding over the crease; lower lids with a soft raised edge; shallow hollows beneath the eyes; a distinct under-eye pouch with a crease below it; fine lines fanning from the outer corners; eyes that sit deeply beneath a strong brow.
- **Nose and midface:** broad bridge with a rounded tip; narrow bridge that widens at the nostrils; nose tip angled slightly to one side; fuller upper cheeks that taper toward the mouth; flatter cheeks with a deeper crease running from the nose to each mouth corner.
- **Mouth and lower face:** narrow upper lip with a fuller lower lip; lips with a softly uneven outline; mouth corners that rest at different heights; short lines dropping from the mouth corners; lower cheeks that hang slightly beside the mouth; a chin that projects beneath a softer jaw; a fold under the chin that blends into the neck.
- **Skin and age:** visible pores; uneven pigmentation; scattered sun spots or small moles; fine creasing around the mouth; crepey skin beneath the eyes; loose neck skin or horizontal neck folds; slight redness or shine. Only use what makes sense for the person's age, skin, and brief.
- **Personal details:** glasses shape and fit; thinning or silver hair at the hairline; a curl falling across the forehead; a gap, crowded teeth, or a slightly uneven tooth edge when teeth are shown. Choose dental details individually, never as an ancestry cue. These can be stronger identity anchors than a generic face-shape label.

## More exact face, skin, and visible body detail

Do not let a racial or ethnic label stand in for color or structure. Within Black American characters alone, range from light golden brown and medium neutral brown to deep brown, dark espresso brown, and very deep neutral brown; describe undertone only when useful. Do not use reddish, red-brown, copper, rust, or red-toned skin descriptions for Black characters. White and Latino characters also have varied light-to-deep shades and undertones. Pick each person's shade individually rather than moving through a fixed palette by group. Do not assign a nose, lip, or eye shape by heritage.

- **Silhouette:** broad forehead narrowing through the temples into a small chin; narrow temples but wide lower cheeks; long rectangular face with a heavy square jaw; round face whose cheeks project beyond the jaw; diamond face with high cheekbones and hollowing below. Say where the width changes, not just the label.
- **Nose:** unusually large nose with a high bridge and convex side profile; long straight projecting bridge with a downward-pointing tip; short broad bridge widening gradually into rounded nostrils; prominent rounded bulb at the tip; narrow upper bridge and broad soft alae; slightly bent bridge. “Greek” or “Roman” can be a user's shorthand, but physical geometry works better in the prompt and does not imply ancestry.
- **Skin surface:** pores visible on nose and inner cheeks; subtle pitted texture from old blemishes; scattered lighter or darker spots; soft matte finish with shine on forehead and nose; fine creasing under eyes; textured temples; beard shadow along the jaw; small neck folds. Choose a few plausible details; do not make everyone blemished, fatigued, or uniformly smooth.
- **Body hair and grooming:** exposed forearms may have fine pale hair, dark dense hair, scattered coarse hair, silvery hair, or very little visible hair. A man's chest hair may show at an open collar when appropriate. Specify facial hair density and edge separately. Body hair is an individual visual choice for any gender.

For each character, describe the **same connected face** from skull outline through eyes and nose to mouth, jaw, neck, and surface, then use a small number of visible body and clothing details. Avoid a catalogue of unrelated feature labels.

## Additional range from the men's examples

The following vocabulary covers younger and older men with different facial structure. These features are **not tied to a race or target audience**. Vary them independently; do not give every man a square face and beard.

- **Head and hairline:** close even hairline; temples beginning to recede; a high hairline with hair remaining at the sides; rounded bald crown; short tight curls with gray at the edges; longer locs with silver through them. Describe the shape of the hairline rather than only “short hair” or “bald.”
- **Brow and eye area:** strong brow ridge over narrow eyes; broad space between brows and upper lids; brows that draw together into a vertical crease; one eye held slightly narrower; fine wrinkles gathered at the outer corner; lower lids that sit flat versus pouches beneath them.
- **Cheeks and face width:** lean cheeks that dip under the cheekbone; full cheeks pushing outward beside a small nose; a wide face whose lower half stays broad; a narrow upper face widening toward the jaw; pronounced cheekbones with a hollow beneath them.
- **Nose and mouth:** a wide nose with a low bridge; a straight bridge with rounded nostrils; a long bridge that projects beyond flatter cheeks; a short wide upper lip; lips pressed into a nearly straight line; a fuller lower lip beneath a defined upper lip. Keep expression separate from permanent structure.
- **Jaw, chin, and neck:** angular jaw with a pointed chin; square chin beneath broad cheeks; rounded chin that merges into the neck; fullness under the chin forming one or more horizontal folds; visible neck creases beneath a lean face. Neck fullness can occur at different body sizes.
- **Facial hair:** close shadow along the jaw; patchy stubble; a short boxed beard following the jaw; full dense beard that widens the lower face; mustache separated from the beard; long wiry salt-and-pepper beard; gray concentrated at the chin or sideburns. Describe its outline, length, and gray pattern instead of just saying “beard.”
- **Skin and age:** textured forehead; weathered cheeks; uneven beard-shadow pigmentation; sun redness; small spots; deeper folds beside the nose; loose skin beneath the jaw. Older faces do not all age through the same features, and a full face need not look tired.

Avoid reading a selfie angle, hard shadow, squint, or smile as a permanent facial trait. The reference sheet should describe the underlying person across different shots.

Describe *where* a feature sits and *how* it changes the silhouette. For example, “her lower cheeks drop into two soft folds beside the mouth, while her chin stays small and rounded” tells more than “round face with jowls.” People may be smiling or serious; do not treat a normal age feature as a problem by itself. For a before/problem character, follow the script's problem and the skill's serious-expression rule.

## Black hairstyle chart — Black characters only

The hairstyle options below are optional choices for Black characters. For an explicitly supplied reference of another character, describe what is visible.

- Women shown: Afro, high puff, box braids, cornrows, Bantu knots, two puffs, curly bob, tapered natural cut, locs, twist-out.
- Men shown: close buzz cut, waves, low fade, short Afro, high-top fade, cornrows, two-strand twists, locs, tapered curls, bald head with a close fade at the sides.
- Vary length, texture, hairline, gray strands, and finish to fit the person's age and brief. Do not give every Black character the same hairstyle. For women, maintain full scalp coverage: no bald spots or visible thinning patches, including on older characters.
